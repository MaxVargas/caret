Categories

19-02-2025

A category C is the data of

  • a collection1 of ’objects’ O(C) ,
  • a collection of ’morphisms’ (or arrows) HomC​(X,Y)={f:X→Y} 2 for each X,Y∈O(C) ,
  • a rule to compose arrows; given f:X→Y and g:Y→Z , then there is an arrow g∘f:X→Z 3,
  • for each X∈O(C) , a distinguished identity morphism 1X​:X→X .

Further, this data has to satisfy the following criteria:

  • (Associativity) We have for any f:W→X,g:X→Y,h:Y→Z , that h∘(g∘f)=(h∘g)∘f) as arrows W→Z .
  • (Unital) For all X,Y∈O(C) and f:X→Y , we have f∘1X​=f . Similarly, for any g:Y→X , we have 1X​∘g=g .

There’s plenty of examples of categories. These two, we’ve already seen:

  • The category, Set, whose objects are (small) sets and morphisms are your typical functions.
  • The category, VecF​ , whose objects are vector spaces over a given field F and morphisms are linear maps.

Here’s a few other examples:

  • Given a (directed) graph G with vertices V and edges E , we can build a category C(G) whose objects are precisely the vertex set V . The morphism set HomCG​(v,w) is precisely the set of paths v⇝w , (including the empty path in the case v=w ).
  • Again consider a graph G . We can build a different category E(G) whose objects are the set of all paths in G , including empty paths (of which there is one for each v∈V ). Morphisms between two paths p:v⇝w and p′:v′⇝w′ will consist of pairs (ψ,φ) where ψ is a path v′⇝v and φ is a path w⇝w′ .
