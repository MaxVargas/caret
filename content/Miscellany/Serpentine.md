I found that the more I wrote, the less I wanted to say. So I'll try to err on the shorter side.

The AI space has grown and (IMO) saturated. After spending a few years at a research firm in the field, I grew disinterested. There's many reasons for this, likely too many for me to document at once. Here's a few reasons:
- In a general sense, I don't know what problem AI is trying to solve. What follows is that basic research in AI loses meaning in any given context.
- The typical use cases of AI leave me uninspired and admittedly disappointed. You can have it make a new image (arguable)... you can have it write a better email... you can have it to plan your vacations... lunch recommendations... These things just don't register as genuine problems in my mind. Obviously AI *can* be genuinely useful in the right setting, but the typical use-cases just aren't it for me...
- I'd rather help solve problems instead of advertise. I recognize this comes off as a jaded opinion about the state of AI/ML research, but I like my lemons sour. I've also got a whole lot to learn about building solutions, but I like learning so that's ok.

I've been learning to be more intentional about the problems I want to solve. When I finished my education, I had a case of burn out[^1]  and convinced myself that I shouldn't be picky and there are many interesting problems out there[^2]. Just needed to get out of academics and AI/ML was my way of getting into the applied sector[^3]. I made the decision in the late summer of 2021, before the popularization of today's genAI systems. Perhaps this is me just trying to resolve my conscience, however. Regardless, it's ok to be picky and try to do something cool.

That said, I don't necessarily disagree with the use of genAI when used responsibly and intentionally. Obviously this can become pretty subjective, but for sure there's lots of irresponsible use of genAI (just as any technology). I do think that AI has brought lots ot the cybersecurity field (good+bad). Of course, there's huge questions on how we can secure genAI systems. There's also lots of application in using genAI systems to solve "classical" problems in cyber. All I'm saying is that it's a 2-way street here. Selfishly, I also view cybersecurity as an area where, at some point, I can reacquaint myself with some nice math.

[^1]: Mainly on my own reckoning. I actually had lots of support and enjoyed working with those I had the opportunity to.
[^2]: There are some problems in AI/ML that I think are interesting and cool from a technical/mathematical perspective, but somehow the (proposed) utility always becomes "we can make a better chat bot with this". Again, not something I really care for.
[^3]: I have the impression that math folks frequently branch out into tech via data science or cryptography. Ironically, I made the choice against pursuing cryptography in order to avoid the cryptobro scene.
