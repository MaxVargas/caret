Anyways, quick points on my research are below, will only write a little here but I'm happy to discuss further. Largely in LLMs (and some vision) consisting of:
- Explainable AI. Things I've done here essentially involve trying to unpack data that gets embedded by foundation models, but there's two spins on what I do here.
	- Studying the embedding spaces of deep neural networks (DNNs) to extract interpretable, explainable, and high-level information about our data. This involves applying dimensionality reduction techniques to see what they tell us about the features of our data. There's a cool application towards understanding the variance between human writing and AI-generated writing in a quantitative sense, and this applies to the image domain too (real images vs AI-generated images). Some people advertise this work has connections to interesting geometric questions about data (e.g., what kind of manifold does natural language data live on?). You can find the preprint [by clicking here](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/2408.10437).
	- Sparse autoencoders and other areas of "mechanistic interpretability". Essentially, rather than use dimensionality reduction, do the opposite to try to achieve the similar means haha. It's much more expensive to do this, however. Anyways, I do work in training these things and some on how we can use them for "model editing" of sorts. Lots of literature here, easier for you to look up big players here (Anthropic, for example) than for me to summarize.
- RAG- and agentic-related applications. In particular been looking at handling knowledge graph (KG) modalities, both generating KGs through LLM-based knowledge discovery or using KGs for Q&A-related tasking. The outlook is that by processing unstructured data (text or image) into a KG, we can encode explicit relationships in our data which an LLM can further exploit downstream. Lots of open questions here beyond just the engineering problem of making things work, from uncertainty quantification to evaluations. This involves lots of playing with different models and the rest of the AI stack.
